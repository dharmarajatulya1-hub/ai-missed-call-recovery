1

Missed-call text-back

Easy · 1 afternoon~$30–100 / moBiggest revenue impact

What's going wrong

After 6 PM and on weekends, calls go to voicemail. Most callers hang up without leaving a message and call a competitor. All three of our test calls ended this way.

What the fix does

When a call goes unanswered, the caller gets a text within seconds:

Sorry we missed you! This is Oak & Ember Heating & Air. Reply here and we'll get you scheduled. If it's an emergency, text URGENT.

The rest of the conversation happens by text. Urgent jobs get flagged to the on-call tech.

Tools

A missed-call text-back service that works with your current number. Many field-service platforms include one, or you can add a standalone tool. No new hardware.

Estimated payoff

40 missed calls/mo × 25% book by text × $380 avg ticket ≈ $3,800 / month
Assumptions: 40 missed calls/month is our guess. Your phone logs will show the real number, and we'll redo this math with it. $380 is a typical HVAC service ticket.

Why it's first: Even at a quarter of this estimate, it covers its own cost in the first week.

2

Review requests + review replies

Easy~$75–300 / mo depending on platformGains build over time

What's going wrong

The first thing new customers see is six unanswered 1-star reviews. Happy customers are never asked to leave a review, so the rating keeps slipping while competitors sit at 4.6★.

What the fix does

Each finished job sends the customer a text asking for a Google review. Each new review comes with a drafted reply you can approve in one tap. Bad reviews get flagged right away so you can reply the same day.

Tools

A review-automation tool that connects to your scheduling software, or the review feature built into it.

Estimated payoff

Target: 3.9★ → 4.4★ in ~6 months Closes most of the gap with competitors
We'll track how many calls come from your Google profile each month (Google Business Profile shows this for free), so you'll see the real effect instead of taking our word for it.

Do this week, free: reply to the six 1-star reviews yourself. We've drafted all six replies in the appendix of your real audit.

3

Online booking for tune-ups & estimates

Medium effort$0 if your software includes itSaves staff time

What's going wrong

The contact form doesn't let anyone actually book. A customer who wants to schedule a tune-up at 10 PM has to wait until someone calls back, and many won't.

What the fix does

Customers can book tune-ups, maintenance visits, and free estimates online. Bookings sync to your techs' calendars. Emergencies still go to the phone.

Tools

The booking feature in your field-service software (Jobber, Housecall Pro, etc.) or a simple scheduler embedded on your site.

Estimated payoff

~5 hrs/week of phone scheduling × 4 weeks ≈ 20 staff hours / month back
You also catch customers who'd rather book online than call, which the phone alone can't do.
4

Quote follow-up on autopilot

EasyOften included with #1's platformMoney you're already owed

What's going wrong

Estimates go out and customers never hear from you again. When we requested one, we got no follow-up at all.

What the fix does

Every undecided quote gets three automatic follow-ups:

Day 1 text: “Any questions on your estimate?”
Day 4 email with financing options
Day 10 “this quote expires soon” reminder

Estimated payoff

25 quotes/mo × 60% unsold = 15 lost
win back 10% of those ≈ 1–2 installs ≈ $6,000–12,000 / month
Assumes quote volume and a $6k average install. Plug in your real numbers and the math adjusts.
5

AI receptionist for after-hours calls

Medium effort~$150–500 / moPhase 2

What's going wrong

Text-back (#1) catches most missed callers. But someone with no heat at 2 AM wants a voice on the line.

What the fix does

An AI phone agent answers after hours, sorts emergencies from routine calls, and books jobs straight into your schedule. Real emergencies are passed to the on-call tech.

When to add it

Decide after 60 days of #1
By then you'll know your real after-hours call volume. If 1–2 emergency jobs a month are slipping through, it pays for itself.
